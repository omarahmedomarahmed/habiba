The verdict, in five lines

What is genuinely good. Consent before recording, a record the patient owns and grants, published prices, a manual rail where nothing moves before a person confirms, audited reads, an honesty check on marketing copy. Competitors do not have most of this. Keep all of it.
What is genuinely broken. Money can be credited without being paid, a signed note can be rewritten, a clinician was silenced in video by my own consent fix, crisis numbers exist for two countries, and several refunds and reports silently do nothing while the screen says they worked.
The pattern behind most defects. A capability was built, then the screen that reaches it, the notice that tells someone, or the check that guards it was not. 14 working capabilities have no screen; 9 pages have no link to them; dozens of actions check only that someone is signed in.
The promise problem. 19 of 25 value statements fail or only partly hold on a real screen today, mostly because copy was written for the product as intended. The rule going forward: build it or delete the sentence, never leave both.
What to do. Fix in four waves (harm, stuck, untrue, missing). Then redesign each portal around the few jobs its person comes to do, using the same design system as the samples you approved.

Four decisions only you can make

Everything else I have decided by default, listed in the plan, each reversible. These four touch privacy promises, legal exposure or who holds authority over money.

1. Names on the company people list (your decision 4). E1 and E2 promise a company never learns who uses the benefit. The research found no therapy benefit (Spring, Modern Health, Lyra, Headspace Care) that shows employers the names of people using care: therapy use is itself the sensitive fact. My recommendation: replace the list with a floored count, and a lookup that finds one named person only to end their benefit when they leave. Today it stays as you decided.
RESEARCH-2 section 4. Spring Health and Modern Health confidentiality pages; Mozilla Foundation 2022 review.
2. The reporting floor for companies. Today any figure is shown once 5 sessions exist. Published norms run from about 5 (survey tools) to 10 (US Medicare, for clinical data, including numbers you can derive by subtraction). For small Egyptian employers I recommend 10, applied to derived figures too, with monthly rather than live refresh.
RESEARCH-2 section 4. ResDAC CMS cell suppression; Culture Amp.
3. Who may approve payouts and clinician licences. Today staff see those queues and every button refuses them, so out of hours nothing moves and the two-person payout rule needs two founders. Options: staff may approve below a threshold with a second approver above it (the maker-checker norm), or the queues are founder-only and hidden from staff. I will make the screens honest either way; granting authority over money and licences is yours.
RESEARCH-2 section 5. NIST AC-5; maker-checker practice.
4. Egypt's data protection regulations. Issued 1 November 2025; the grace period ends around 31 October 2026. Health data needs explicit written consent, a licence and an accredited DPO, and sending audio or notes abroad for AI processing needs a separate cross-border licence. This is legal work, not code, but it decides where transcription can run. I recommend a lawyer before launch.
RESEARCH-2 section 3. Chambers, Kennedys, Al Tamimi 2025 to 2026.

Legal and compliance flags

AI scribe consent. 2025 to 2026 class actions (Sharp with Abridge, then Sutter and MemorialCare) turned on recording without all-party consent and on consent text written into charts. Our consent is patient-led already; the partner API writes consent text into the draft, which is being removed now.
Psychotherapy notes. Under HIPAA they are a separate object, excluded from access and exports. If clinicians write private process notes, they need their own place that never reaches exports, employers, partners or the patient view.
Right to amend. A patient may ask for an amendment; the clinician decides within 60 days and a refusal keeps the patient's statement of disagreement. Today a patient can flag a document but not a diagnosis, and flags cannot be withdrawn.
"HIPAA BAA included". No BAA exists anywhere in the product. The claim is being removed; offering one is a legal task.
Accessibility. The US Section 504 deadline moved to 11 May 2027. Building to WCAG 2.2 AA now covers it.
Crisis numbers. Every number and its hours must be re-verified before launch and kept as configuration an operator can update without a release.
