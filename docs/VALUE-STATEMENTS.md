# What we promise, and what proves it

🔴 **Generated by `npm run prove` from `scripts/_value-statements.ts`. Do not edit it by hand.**

25 promises, five per audience, and every one of them is a claim this
product already makes on a page a stranger can read or a rule it already enforces in code.
Nothing here is new. `where` names the place we already say it, and that constraint is the
whole point: a test plan written against invented promises proves a product nobody was sold.

`docs/PROVE-IT.md` is the walk. This is the list it is walked against.

## The two claims that may never appear here

`lib/content/honesty.ts` refuses both at the door, in `savePage` and again against the
published rows:

1. **That paid sessions cover our fee.** At the prices this is actually sold at, fifteen per
   cent of a twenty dollar session is three dollars against a four dollar fee. What is true is
   netting, which is `T3`.
2. **Any forecast of what a clinician will earn.** That is a prediction about somebody else's
   business, and a clinician who moves practice on the strength of one has been misled by us.

## How to read a row

**Says** is the promise. **Where** is where we already make it. **Proof** is what has to be
visible on a screen, to a person, for it to be kept. Not a row somebody could query: the
whole reason eight people walk this rather than a verifier is that a verifier holds an
invariant about a row and cannot tell you the screen above it does not say enough for
somebody to act on.

## Somebody in therapy

Signs in at `/patient/login`. 2 testers.

### P1 · Pick someone free, pay, and you are in a session.

**Where we say it.** `/`, the radar card: “Somebody who is free now”

**Proved when.** From the app's home screen to a live video room, counting the taps out loud. Every step that is not picking, paying or going in is the finding.

### P2 · Nothing the product tells you is only in an email.

**Where we say it.** `scripts/verify-notices.ts`, the gate written after a patient was invited on production and their app said nothing

**Proved when.** An invitation, a payment confirmation and a session starting each appear in the app itself, with the session orb on every screen while money is owed or a door is open.

### P3 · Nothing written by a machine reaches you unsigned.

**Where we say it.** `/for-patients`, “You never talk to the AI”

**Proved when.** The summary after a session carries a clinician's name and credentials. Before they sign it, the app says they are still writing it rather than showing a draft.

### P4 · One record, however many therapists. Every version stays, under its author's name.

**Where we say it.** `/for-patients`, “It moves with you”

**Proved when.** A record seen by two clinicians at two practices shows both summaries, each named, and the patient decides who may read the history.

### P5 · A crisis path that never depends on money.

**Where we say it.** C235, and `components/patient/session-orb.tsx`, which sits UNDER the SOS orb on purpose

**Proved when.** With an unpaid session and a payment reminder on screen, the SOS button is reachable, on top, and dials without passing anything about money.

## A clinician, solo or on a clinic seat

Signs in at `/login`. 3 testers: one on pay as you go, two on clinic seats.

### T1 · The note, before you stand up. Written from what was actually said.

**Where we say it.** `/`, the how-it-works card

**Proved when.** A session ends and the draft is there, built from the transcript, not from a template. It says draft on every screen until it is signed.

### T2 · Take it off the record for a minute and nothing in that minute is kept.

**Where we say it.** `/`, the room card

**Proved when.** The transcript has a hole where the off-record minute was, and the note does not describe it.

### T3 · What you owe comes out of what you earn before it reaches your account.

**Where we say it.** `lib/content/honesty.ts`: netting is the true version of the claim it refuses. `/pricing`

**Proved when.** The earnings screen shows held earnings and what is owed as two halves of one number, and the payout is the difference.

### T4 · Invite somebody to a session and the link works, for a stranger or for a patient who is already signed in.

**Where we say it.** Task 109, and `app/join/[token]/page.tsx`, which stopped asking a signed-in patient their own name

**Proved when.** The same invitation, opened by a stranger, asks a name; opened by the patient it belongs to, says “Joining as …” and asks nothing.

### T5 · Only a clinician the patient chose can ask the copilot anything, only about them, and only while they allow it.

**Where we say it.** `/for-patients`, “You never talk to the AI”

**Proved when.** The copilot answers with the sentence it came from attached, and a revoked grant stops it on the next question rather than at the next session.

## The person who runs a practice

Signs in at `/clinic/sign-in`. 1 tester, the person who runs the practice.

### C1 · Add a clinician and they reach the radar once their licence is verified.

**Where we say it.** `/`, the clinic card: “Seats and the people on them”

**Proved when.** A seat added in the clinic portal puts that clinician on the public radar once verified, with their verification state on the row.

### C2 · The practice sees a patient as a first name and a last initial, and nothing clinical.

**Where we say it.** The founder's decision of 2026-09-23: the practice sees first name and last initial on each clinician's calendar and patient list

**Proved when.** In the clinic portal a patient appears only as a first name and a last initial, on a clinician's calendar or patient list. No note, transcript, risk, diagnosis, phone or email on any screen.

### C3 · One bill for the practice, not one per clinician.

**Where we say it.** `/`, “One set of books”

**Proved when.** One invoice, priced per seat, and the seats on it are the seats that were filled.

### C4 · A seat that leaves mid-month lowers the next bill by exactly one seat, and that clinician keeps working.

**Where we say it.** `docs/simulation/09-THE-EDGES.md` `PL6`

**Proved when.** Release a seat: the next bill is lower by one seat and the clinician lands on pay-as-you-go by themselves. Nobody is suspended.

### C5 · Earnings per clinician, because you pay them.

**Where we say it.** `/`, “One set of books”

**Proved when.** Each clinician's earnings are visible to the practice, and each clinician's patients appear only as a first name and a last initial, with nothing clinical.

## An employer paying for staff therapy

Signs in at `/sponsor/sign-in`. 1 tester, the person who funds the pot.

### E1 · Who is enrolled, what you funded, and every session's money: its price, your cover, your share and your employee's share. Never who, never which therapist, never the day.

**Where we say it.** `/`, the company card: “The pot, and what is left in it”, widened by the founder's decisions of 2026-09-23 and 2026-09-24: companies see their employees' names and control their benefit, and see each session's money with no name and no therapist

**Proved when.** `/sponsor` lists enrolled names with benefit controls, and a ledger of session money entries (price, cover %, covered amount, employee's share) with analytics, filters, sorting and export, carrying no employee, no therapist and no date finer than the publishing batch. The balance is a published figure rather than a live one.

### E2 · The portal has no screen that could show a note, a session time or an attendance list.

**Where we say it.** `/`, “What you will never see”. A fact about the queries rather than a promise

**Proved when.** Every screen in the company portal, opened one by one, with nothing clinical on any of them. An employee's name appears only on the enrolment list; if it appears beside a therapist, a session or a date anywhere, the walk stops there.

### E3 · A price somebody was shown is a price they are owed.

**Where we say it.** `docs/simulation/09-THE-EDGES.md` `CV7`

**Proved when.** Lower coverage after a session is booked: that session still splits at the old share, and the next one is offered at the new one.

### E4 · Setting coverage to zero is not removing somebody.

**Where we say it.** C345, `docs/simulation/09-THE-EDGES.md` `CV6`

**Proved when.** At 0% the employee keeps their badge and their place on the roster, owes the whole price, and no screen says they were removed.

### E5 · When the pot runs out the patient is told to ask HR, not shown a payment error.

**Where we say it.** `docs/simulation/09-THE-EDGES.md` `CV9`

**Proved when.** A booking against an empty pot: the pot takes nothing, the ordinary pay link is offered, and the patient's screen says who to ask.

## Us: approving, confirming, monitoring

Signs in at `/staff/sign-in`. 1 tester, doing the work of all our staff.

### A1 · Nothing is granted before a person confirms it.

**Where we say it.** `verify:rail`. There is no processor behind the Egyptian rail

**Proved when.** Money submitted sits in a queue until an operator presses Confirm, and the thing it pays for does not start until then.

### A2 · Pressing Confirm twice moves the money once.

**Where we say it.** `docs/simulation/09-THE-EDGES.md` `RA3`, held by `verify:edges`

**Proved when.** Confirm, watch nothing obvious change, confirm again. No second ledger leg, no second settlement, and the screen says which.

### A3 · A rejection is a sentence in the operator's own words, and the payer reads it verbatim.

**Where we say it.** `docs/simulation/09-THE-EDGES.md` `RA4`

**Proved when.** Reject a transfer with a specific reason, then read that exact sentence on the payer's own screen.

### A4 · Money that arrives with no claim is a line somebody has to decide about, never silently kept.

**Where we say it.** `docs/simulation/09-THE-EDGES.md` `RA6`, `RA8`

**Proved when.** An overpayment and an unmatchable bank line both appear on `/admin/transfers` as work, with the difference visible.

### A5 · A role is a list, not a rank, and every read is written down.

**Where we say it.** `/security`

**Proved when.** A staff account is refused a founder-only screen and redirected rather than shown an error, and the refusal is on the record.

## The five positions

Each is a complete starting position rather than a change to the last one, so there is no
order dependence between them and a half-finished walk cannot poison the next.

```
npm run on:production -- verify:demo -- --scenario=<name>
```

### `live`

It is the flagship, it touches six surfaces, and every part of it was broken on production within the last week: the patient was told nothing, the pay link asked a signed-in person their own name, and the room threw a client-side exception the clinician could not read.

The company covers **60 per cent** and the pot was funded with **EGP 7,500** ($150 at 50 to the dollar), on top of a $100 welcome credit. One person is on that pot, and one patient is enrolled nowhere.

### `money`

The path with the most moving parts in this product. Sprint 76 found four defects in it in one afternoon, and all four were invisible to every gate that claimed to cover it.

The company covers **10 per cent** and the pot was funded with **EGP 7,500** ($150 at 50 to the dollar), on top of a $100 welcome credit. Two people are on that pot.

### `continuity`

Portability is the claim the whole patient site rests on, and it is the one thing that cannot be shown on a database where everybody saw one person.

The company covers **60 per cent** and the pot was funded with **EGP 7,500** ($150 at 50 to the dollar), on top of a $100 welcome credit. One person is on that pot, and one patient is enrolled nowhere.

### `crisis`

C235 is that a patient's crisis path never depends on money. The rest of this position is the honest half: the rejected transfer, the expired claim link and the applicant who is waiting, which are the three ways this product currently strands a person.

The company covers **60 per cent** and the pot was funded with **EGP 7,500** ($150 at 50 to the dollar), on top of a $100 welcome credit. One person is on that pot, and one patient is enrolled nowhere.

### `growth`

Everything that changes shape rather than state. The pot emptying mid-week and the seat released mid-month are the two the product has never been walked through.

The company covers **100 per cent** and the pot was funded with **EGP 5,000** ($100 at 50 to the dollar), with no welcome credit. Two people are on that pot.
